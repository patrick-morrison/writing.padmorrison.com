# Day at the beach

Source: https://writing.padmorrison.com/day-at-the-beach/
Published: 2023-02-10T06:50:46.000Z
Updated: 2023-02-10T09:44:34.000Z

Bunuru is the best time of year to be at the beach. This is [the Noongar season around February and March](https://www.vincent.wa.gov.au/our-neighbourhood/community/culture/aboriginal-culture/vincent-reconciliation.aspx), with warm morning easterlies and a cool afternoon sea breeze. The water is warm after months of hot days, and the reliable easterlies keep the water clear.

This week's snorkel spot is Cottesloe Beach, with stunning snorkelling just offshore. For the divers, Point Peron is a more adventurous spot, with hundreds of caves to explore - but it's also great for an easy snorkel.

## Cottesloe Beach

*Cottesloe. Depth 1-5m. Snorkel.
Low swell (less than a metre). Easterly wind.*

Cottesloe needs no introduction, it's the most iconic beach in Perth. The gorgeous reef system just off the sand makes it an ideal pre-work snorkel spot. There are three key areas for snorkelling from the main beach: the north reef, just south of the groyne, or inside the shark net.

> Image: [0FA25402-7B46-43DD-B512-D4D340017A60.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/0FA25402-7B46-43DD-B512-D4D340017A60.jpeg) - Schools of pufferfish on the southern Cottesloe reef.

The north reef is your best default. There's [a small sheltered lagoon](https://goo.gl/maps/Vkf7PUkth3CxQPJr5?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) surrounded by shallow limestone reef and seagrass meadows. Here you'll find all the usual Perth sea life - it's very similar to[Mettams Pool](https://writing.padmorrison.com/creatures/).

> Image: [68AF3B44-BAD7-4792-BEA8-77AA7923D7C7.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/68AF3B44-BAD7-4792-BEA8-77AA7923D7C7.jpeg) - Image: [638EBF40-E609-4FC6-96C7-172D9DFD191C.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/638EBF40-E609-4FC6-96C7-172D9DFD191C.jpeg) - Snorkelling the shallows of north Cottesloe reef

My favourite spot is on the other side of the shark net, [just south of the groyne](https://goo.gl/maps/mDQmzKVb8PSPMKJw6?ref=writing.padmorrison.com). Large schools of fish congregate around the groyne and in the slightly deeper waters beyond the drop-off. You can follow the reef south for a little way, and then follow it back. Be careful of fish hooks, and the breaking swell on the reef.

> Image: [A79FB6A9-9F75-4E03-94D3-D5B7FF2037C9.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/A79FB6A9-9F75-4E03-94D3-D5B7FF2037C9.jpeg) - Image: [8FBAB6F5-4177-4F18-B776-69853AD83409.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/8FBAB6F5-4177-4F18-B776-69853AD83409.jpeg) - Larger schools of fish and stingrays beyond the groyne, to the south of Cottesloe Beach.

While most sea life hangs out on the reef, there is plenty to see inside the shark net. Schools of fish can be seen over the shallow sand, along the rocks, or along the net itself.

After snorkelling, there are a lot of nice cafes to try nearby - a pre-work favourite is a breakfast wrap from Old Buoy under the tearooms. If you go for a sunset snorkel, the pizza place nearby has blankets and hot chocolates.

## Point Peron

*Rockingham. Depth 1-10m. Dive or snorkel.
Reasonable swell, easterly winds.*

Point Peron is a classic of Perth diving and snorkelling. On a calm day, it's a perfect spot for finding creatures and swimming through limestone caves. The other main draw is the larger animals - dolphins are almost always hanging around, and in summer hammerhead sharks school over sandy areas.

> Image: [0C049A95-9A53-40DC-8C04-3E49F9CD333D.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/0C049A95-9A53-40DC-8C04-3E49F9CD333D.jpeg) - A swim-through at Point Peron.

It's hard to give directions for diving here, because everywhere is good. There's a main carpark [over a sheltered bay](https://goo.gl/maps/HtvQqXbymjh7pp7r8?ref=writing.padmorrison.com), with lots of reef to explore. Further out are sandy patches and larger caves - take your pick! If you have a mate who wants to stay dry - there are sandy beaches to lay on, and limestone cliffs to wander.

> Image: [CF8CB13E-BF12-4E75-B409-3C63B3F2F69B.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/CF8CB13E-BF12-4E75-B409-3C63B3F2F69B.jpeg) - Image: [B6361949-71B6-43B5-85C8-1A210A0CE1C4.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/B6361949-71B6-43B5-85C8-1A210A0CE1C4.jpeg) - [A nudibranch (sea slug)](https://www.mycrittercompendium.com/opisthobranchs?ref=writing.padmorrison.com#/chromodoris-westraliensis/) on the shallow reef at Point Peron (left). Searching for bigger creatures on the sandflats (right).

You'll notice it's a very popular dive site, despite being shallow enough to snorkel. Many of the smaller creatures, such as nudibranchs and octopuses are much easier to find when you have your own air! If you're looking to identify what you see, [My Critter Compendium](https://www.mycrittercompendium.com/?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) is a great site run by local divers, with very reliable and clear information.

> Image: [619EF3D3-C76C-417D-8F1F-6F4B197FB578.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/619EF3D3-C76C-417D-8F1F-6F4B197FB578.jpeg) - Snorkellers, divers, stand-up paddle-boarders, kayakers and beachgoers all happily coexist at Point Peron.

# Wadjemup Rottnest Limestone

Source: https://writing.padmorrison.com/rottnest-limestone/
Published: 2023-02-02T00:49:07.000Z
Updated: 2023-02-10T09:47:32.000Z

The clear waters of Wadjemup Rottnest Island are full of limestone reef. This limestone was formed on land, when the sea level was much lower. Today it provides an important habitat for sea life, and gorgeous caves for divers to explore.

Little Salmon bay is a popular snorkel spot, with an informative underwater trail. Roe Reef has some of the best diving in the area, with an enormous cave at its centre.

## Little Salmon Bay

*Rottnest Island south. Depth 1-5m. Snorkel.
Most conditions, northerly wind best.*

Little Salmon Bay is on the southern side of Wadjemup Rottnest Island. The bay is sheltered by a limestone reef, making it a popular snorkelling spot. It's a 25 minute bike ride from the settlement.

> Image: [AE66DB90-E5BF-4A87-8CDB-AC62EA9C5EE0.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/AE66DB90-E5BF-4A87-8CDB-AC62EA9C5EE0.jpeg) - Pink coral at Little Salmon Bay, near an information plaque.

The waters of Wadjemup are typically much clearer than Perth coastal waters, because it's further from the murky Swan River sediment. The benefit of an island is that one side is always sheltered from the wind - at this south-facing bay a northerly wind is ideal. Make sure you get there early - by mid-morning there can be huge crowds.

The warm Leeuwin Current supports a surprisingly tropical ecosystem for this latitude. Big schools of fish are always swimming through the pink Pocillopora corals. Eagle rays can be seen on the sand patches, and in the seagrass meadows to the east.

The best way to snorkel the bay is to follow the snorkel trail, which was recently upgraded by the Rottnest Island Authority. These plinths contain information about the bay and the sea life around you. The ten buoys are spaced pretty evenly apart, so you can follow them around to see all the different habitats.

> Image: [4CE7C0DC-E4BF-473A-A829-AA25A1C3B00F.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/4CE7C0DC-E4BF-473A-A829-AA25A1C3B00F.jpeg) - Image: [82BD37BA-21FF-4E02-B5D8-950EC24BBA0C.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/82BD37BA-21FF-4E02-B5D8-950EC24BBA0C.jpeg) - Southern eagle ray on sand (left). Snorkeller reading information on the snorkel trail (right).

The area is an excellent example of the Tamala limestone formation, which runs all along the Western Australian coast. Limestone pinnacles, now underwater, are evidence of changing ancient sea levels - they were formed by large tree roots when the sea level was much lower.

If you're interested in the geological history of the island, [the Geological Survey of Western Australia has an interactive map on their website](https://www.dmp.wa.gov.au/Geological-Survey/Geology-of-Rottnest-Island-16421.aspx?ref=writing.padmorrison.com). Here you can also download the 'Guidebook to the geology of Rottnest Island' by Phillip Playford, for an even more detailed look.

> Image: [AA534ED6-FF5D-4208-A4A3-DC1CE86362DB.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/AA534ED6-FF5D-4208-A4A3-DC1CE86362DB.jpeg) - Limestone pinnacles at Little Salmon Bay.

## Roe Reef

*Rottnest Island north. Depth 5-18m. Dive.
Reasonable swell, southerly wind.*

Roe Reef was my first-ever boat dive, on my Open Water Scuba course in 2015. To be honest, I didn't see very much. Since then I've revisited it many times, and it's become one of my favourite sites.

The main cave is the most spectacular feature. In the south-facing ridgeline there are many small caves and swim throughs, but this one is huge. You enter an arch from the sand patch, at about 12m depth, and it opens up into an enormous space.

> Image: [22306485-1AC9-4D63-A128-E66ED6C44752.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/22306485-1AC9-4D63-A128-E66ED6C44752.jpeg) - Image: [0719A50E-EC37-4D40-8292-5C745191B61E.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/0719A50E-EC37-4D40-8292-5C745191B61E.jpeg) - Roe Reef main cave on a UWAUC dive. Diver for scale.

These caves were all formed when the area was dry land - some time before the seas rose to their current position 7 thousand years ago. Roe Reef is what remains of an ancient parabolic sand dune. In the [Department of Transport Lidar](https://catalogue.data.wa.gov.au/dataset/composite-surfaces-multibeam-lidar-laser?ref=writing.padmorrison.com), you can see the cemented sand dunes branching off the main rise. These ancient dunes surround the island, leaving many caves for divers to explore.

> Image: [A8B37233-5CD8-496C-B5BE-740EFE68FCC3.png](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/A8B37233-5CD8-496C-B5BE-740EFE68FCC3.png) - Roe Reef on the [Department of Transport Lidar](https://catalogue.data.wa.gov.au/dataset/composite-surfaces-multibeam-lidar-laser?ref=writing.padmorrison.com), compared to the Lancelin sand dunes on Bing Images. Rottnest basemap from Sentinel satellite.

Before the sea level rose, the ancestors of Noongar people lived in this place. They would have used the caves for shelter. Wadjemup was only half-way out to the ice-age shoreline, and the island would have been a prominent hill on the otherwise relatively flat coastal plain. I like to imagine people standing under the arch, and wonder what the space meant at the time. Noongar people remain connected to these places, and have stories of when you could walk across the landscape.

> Image: [5B05E69E-50C7-42DA-A8F0-9E4D5774692E.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/02/5B05E69E-50C7-42DA-A8F0-9E4D5774692E.jpeg) - The submerged limestone landscapes of Roe Reef.

# Club dives

Source: https://writing.padmorrison.com/club-dives/
Published: 2023-01-19T01:00:21.000Z
Updated: 2023-02-01T13:34:42.000Z

If you are looking for dive buddies, you should join a club dive. There are free shore dives every week, normally advertised on Facebook. [UWA Underwater Club](https://www.facebook.com/groups/229087117103242) and [the DiveTub](https://www.facebook.com/DiveTub) run free night dives on Wednesday nights, and dive shops like [Dolphin Scuba](https://www.facebook.com/DolphinScubaPerth)and [Perth Scuba](https://www.facebook.com/PerthScuba) offer Sunday morning dives. These often come with a free BBQ...

This week the sites are classic club dive spots - safe, approachable and reliable. You also don't have to wait for a club - head along when it's a bit quieter! Kwinana Grain Terminal is easily one of the most popular sites in Perth, and the Coombe is diveable every day of the year.

## Kwinana Grain Terminal

*Cockburn Sound. Depth 2-15m. Snorkel or Dive.
Reasonable swell. Easterly or southerly wind.*

Kwinana Grain Terminal is a working jetty in Rockingham, where grain is loaded onto ships for export. Fortunately, the 800m long jetty is also accessible for almost its entire length - more than you can do in one dive.

> Image: [DSC00005-KwinanaGrainTerminal-20220123-pmSonyUW-0008-edit-1.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00005-KwinanaGrainTerminal-20220123-pmSonyUW-0008-edit-1.jpg) - Image: [DSC00042-KwinanaGrainTerminal-20220123-pmSonyUW-0045-edit-3.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00042-KwinanaGrainTerminal-20220123-pmSonyUW-0045-edit-3.jpg) - Life at Kwinana Grain Terminal. Sponges and smaller critters on the pylons, and fish in between.

Conditions can vary, but it is almost always diveable. The site is best for small sea life, which makes it a staple for macro photographers. All the usual jetty life is here, including very photogenic nudibranchs and octopuses.

There is a [public carpark right on the terminal](https://goo.gl/maps/b3hjmiYG3WNVRXCj9?ref=writing.padmorrison.com), where you can gear up. It's a short walk down, and a short swim out to diveable depths. Snorkellers would also enjoy the shallows. It's mostly the same for the entire length - so take it slow and look carefully.

> Image: [DSC00011-KwinanaGrainTerminal-20220226-pmUWSony-0011-edit-1.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00011-KwinanaGrainTerminal-20220226-pmUWSony-0011-edit-1.jpg) - Image: [DSC00016-KwinanaGrainTerminal-20220226-pmUWSony-0016-edit-1.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00016-KwinanaGrainTerminal-20220226-pmUWSony-0016-edit-1.jpg) - Octopus hiding in a den under Kwinana Grain Terminal.

In the last hundred metres it gets much deeper and darker. You won't make a return trip on a single tank if you start at the shore, so surface swim a few hundred metres out if seeing this is your aim. It gets colder and much deeper - but there are large schools of fish to see. I've always turned back before reaching the very end - it really is quite spooky down there after a few minutes. If you brave it, best to bring a torch.

However you dive it, turn back at half-air and swim back underwater. It always seems to be the way back when you see bigger fish and rays!

> Image: [335ab35a21c0f6bdca8b02fbfdf94c57.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/335ab35a21c0f6bdca8b02fbfdf94c57.jpeg) - Watching a ray beyond the shelter of the jetty.

## The Coombe Shallow Wrecks

*Swan River. Depth 7-12m. Dive.
All conditions.*

The Coombe Shallow wrecks are the remains of motorboats, now resting on the riverbed off Mosman Park. It's a popular training and club dive site because it's shallow, not subject to ocean conditions, and there's some interesting things to see.

> Image: [DJI_0073-CoombeWrecksploration3DTraining-20221113-MavicMinipm-0002.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DJI_0073-CoombeWrecksploration3DTraining-20221113-MavicMinipm-0002.jpg) - The Coombe foreshore, with carpark entry and moored boats.

A pole trail from shore now connects the two motorboats, a dinghy, and a seahorse habitat. The motorboats are both in 9m of water, and Coombe Shallow Wreck 2 is just 10m north of mooring 754. It can get very murky, but you can always find your way with the pole trail, and by watching your depth! For more information, [Dave Jackson has the detailed practical guide to diving these shipwrecks](https://www.divingwawrecks.com/coombe-reserve?ref=writing.padmorrison.com).

> Image: [DSC00210-CoombeWrecksploration3DTraining-20221113-SonyUW-0001.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00210-CoombeWrecksploration3DTraining-20221113-SonyUW-0001.jpg) - Image: [DSC00292-CoombeWrecksploration3DTraining-20221113-SonyUW-0001-1.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00292-CoombeWrecksploration3DTraining-20221113-SonyUW-0001-1.jpg) - Tech divers from the [Wrecksploration team](https://www.facebook.com/WreckSploration) practicing recording Coombe Shallow Wreck 2 using 3D photogrammetry.

This site is where I first properly practiced my underwater photogrammetry skills. The poor visibility means you rarely see the shipwrecks in their entirety, until you 3D scan them piece by piece and view them on your computer. Below is an image of the Coombe Shallow Wreck 2 accross 3 different years. Notice the engine block, partly intact hull, debris field and barrels. Also note the change to the site over time - particularly the end near the barrels that was damaged between 2018 and 2019.

> Image: [coombe2-text.png](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/coombe2-text.png) - 3D model of Coombe Shallow Wreck 2.

If you are looking to practice other skills, such as navigation or macro photography (of sea horses!), then the Coombe is always a good bet. If you're looking for something deeper, the Coombe Deep wrecks are a short swim away.

# Diving way down south

Source: https://writing.padmorrison.com/diving-way-down-south/
Published: 2023-01-12T01:00:15.000Z
Updated: 2023-01-18T12:51:20.000Z

Western Australia's southern coast is perhaps best known for surfing, but there is also excellent diving and snorkelling to be found. Green's Pool in William Bay National Park is one of the most beginner-friendly snorkelling sites out there. The Herschel wreck off Albany is a rarely-dived wreck, and perfect for the adventurous diver.

## Green's Pool and Madfish Bay

*William Bay National Park. Depth 1-4m. Snorkel.
Most conditions, reasonable swell.*

Green's Pool and Madfish Bay are just off William Bay National Park, 20 minutes drive west of Denmark. The granite boulders shelter the waters inside from the energetic Southern Ocean.

> Image: [DSC00129-DenmarkGreensPool-20230106-pmSonyUW-0646-edit-2.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00129-DenmarkGreensPool-20230106-pmSonyUW-0646-edit-2.jpg) - A school of fish in Green's Pool.

The pools feel like an aquarium, with clear waters and plenty of fish. We visited last week as a storm blew in, and beneath the waves it was serene.

The granite is fun to explore, and some boulders have sheer sides deep enough to jump from. It's a safe and approachable snorkel - with the crowds to match. You should arrive very early to get a park close by.

> Image: [DSC00155-DenmarkGreensPool-20230106-pmSonyUW-0672-edit-1.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00155-DenmarkGreensPool-20230106-pmSonyUW-0672-edit-1.jpg) - Image: [DSC00073-DenmarkGreensPool-20230106-pmSonyUW-0590-edit-1.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00073-DenmarkGreensPool-20230106-pmSonyUW-0590-edit-1.jpg) - William Bay National Park is full of granite boulders.

A couple of minutes down the road is Madfish Bay, which is equally protected by granite barriers. If you are visiting Green's Pool, stop by here as well. It is a bit quieter, which can be your lifeline for finding a parking spot. You can swim on either side of the sand bank, or wade across and explore the island.

> Image: [D371AB08-2512-488F-A633-9D0FF3C995D0.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/D371AB08-2512-488F-A633-9D0FF3C995D0.jpeg) - Madfish Bay from above - smaller crowds but the same experience.

## Herschel Shipwreck

*Little Island, Two Peoples Bay. Depth 16-22m. Dive.
Low swell (less than a metre). *

Herschel was an iron sailing ship, built in 1857. In the 1870s it was used to carry immigrants to Queensland, and following that it was used as a coal hulk around Albany. In 1908 it was deliberately sunk [off the eastern slope of Inner Island](https://goo.gl/maps/S1g5KgR3rrEEPTPB9?ref=writing.padmorrison.com), east of Albany.

It isn't a major dive attraction - but don't let that stop you. It should be dived on an advanced ticket due to the depth, but it's a beautiful and sheltered site of historical interest.

> Image: [pm-herchel-20191126-bow-12.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/pm-herchel-20191126-bow-12.jpg) - Image: [pm-herchel-20191126-bow-7-3.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/pm-herchel-20191126-bow-7-3.jpg) - Bow section of Herschel, resting on sand in 22m of water. Pictured is MAAWA volunteer Shannon Reid, with a scale bar. Patrick Morrison/WA Museum.

The wreck is almost entirely broken up, but it is still recognisable as a vessel about 50m in length. The bow section has broken off entirely and lies on the sandy bottom. The rest of the hull is collapsed over the slope, with the stern at the other end. On the stern, you can see supports for a lower and upper deck - consistent with Herschel's historically recorded structure.

> Image: [pm-herchel-20191126-hull-on-rocks-2.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/pm-herchel-20191126-hull-on-rocks-2.jpg) - Collapsed hull of Herschel on rocks. Patrick Morrison/WA Museum.

To find the wreck, you will have to boat out to Inner Island following the [Museum's coordinates](https://museum.wa.gov.au/maritime-archaeology-db/wrecks/herschel-ex-edith-byrne?ref=writing.padmorrison.com). There is a distinctive vein in the granite, which marks the site. Follow the slope down from there and you should find it.

> Image: [pm-herchel-20191126-stern-4.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/pm-herchel-20191126-stern-4.jpg) - Stern section of Herschel, showing structural evidence of multiple decks. Patrick Morrison/WA Museum.

The wreck was rediscovered in 2018 by Marc Payne, who was working in the area as an abalone diver. Marc is an incredibly interesting guy - he spends more time underwater than almost anyone else, and [dives in a scooter-powered shark cage](https://www.watoday.com.au/national/western-australia/the-wa-abalone-diver-capturing-close-encounters-with-white-sharks-20160930-grsiem.html?ref=writing.padmorrison.com).

> Image: [pm-herchel-20191126--1.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/pm-herchel-20191126--1.jpg) - Discoverer Marc Payne diving on the kelp slopes. Patrick Morrison/WA Museum.

This site is special to me because I volunteered on the [WA Museum's shipwreck inspection](https://museum.wa.gov.au/maritime-archaeology-db/sites/default/files/no._332_herschel_and_albany_site_inspections_november_2019.pdf?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) when this site was first reported, the summer before I started working there as a maritime archeologist. We mapped the site out, measured some features, and recorded the bow and stern using 3D photogrammetry.

Any newly-located shipwrecks in Western Australia are inspected by the Museum, so they can be mapped and identified. If you ever find a shipwreck - now you know who to call!

# Diving down south

Source: https://writing.padmorrison.com/diving-down-south/
Published: 2023-01-05T01:00:03.000Z
Updated: 2023-01-05T09:59:18.000Z

Like most of Perth, I'm spending my New Year's holidays down south. This week's sites are Busselton Jetty and the HMAS Swan shipwreck. Both can be done as day-trips from Perth, or part of a weekend getaway.

Busselton Jetty offers some of the most vibrant and clear-watered jetty diving in Western Australia. The HMAS Swan wreck is huge, and much more intact than any of the wrecks around the Perth metro area - with opportunity to safely explore inside.

## Busselton Jetty

*Busselton. Depth 9m. Snorkel or Dive.
Reasonable swell.*

Busselton Jetty is the the longest timber jetty in the southern hemisphere. There is a dive platform at the very end, 1.8km out to sea. It is one of my favourite day trips, at just 2.5 hours drive south of Perth.

> Image: [DSC00174-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0173-edit.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00174-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0173-edit.jpg) - Image: [DSC00237-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0236-edit.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00237-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0236-edit.jpg) - [Scorpion fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorpaena_sumptuosa) (left), corals on the jetty pylons (right)

The jetty pylons are covered with coral, and the shelter of the jetty is home to abundant sea life. The Leeuwin Current brings warm water from the north, sustaining a remarkably tropical ecosystem for such a southerly latitude. For friends that don't dive, they can still join by touring the underwater observatory, right next to the dive platform.

The walk is the most challenging feature of this dive, especially if you are carrying dive gear. You can hire trolleys to help cart everything or buy a [beach trolley from Kmart](https://www.kmart.com.au/product/foldable-beach-trolley-42365587/?ref=writing.padmorrison.com). It takes ~25 minutes each way, and you need to pay a $4 entry fee.

> Image: [DSC00124-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0123-edit-2.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00124-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0123-edit-2.jpg) - Image: [DSC00180-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0179-edit-2.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00180-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0179-edit-2.jpg) - Diver ladder (left) and freedivers enjoying the shallow, clear waters (right).

If you are in Busselton for a holiday, make sure you book a day for it. To day-trip it from Perth, drive down early in the morning, do a dive or two, and then return in the afternoon. Before you plan the long drive down, check the conditions using the [live webcam from the underwater observator](https://www.busseltonjetty.com.au/live-webcam/?ref=writing.padmorrison.com)y.

Busselton Jetty was my first night dive, and is still my favourite. It is easy to navigate, and much of the marine life is more active at night. The sand in Geographe Bay is host to bioluminescent plankton: if you stir it up with your fins, it will sparkle like the night sky. Dive it first during the day, then return that evening for a very different experience.

> Image: [DSC00103-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0102-edit.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00103-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0102-edit.jpg) - Image: [DSC00090-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0089-edit.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/DSC00090-BusseltonJetty-20211204-pmSony-0089-edit.jpg) - Octopus camofaluged in the jetty (left), light filtering throught the jetty from above (right).

## HMAS Swan

*Eagle Bay. Depth 32m. Boat dive.*

The HMAS Swan is a purposely-sunk dive wreck off the coast of Dunsborough, not far from Busselton. It rests in just over 30m of water, and can be entered by properly-trained divers. The ship was a 113m long destroyer escort, belonging to the Australian Navy.

> Image: [6bdf821f89d8e5de6c498f2c584e37b4.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/6bdf821f89d8e5de6c498f2c584e37b4.jpeg) - Image: [e9a935f8d4a25e83219331194419958c.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/e9a935f8d4a25e83219331194419958c.jpeg) - Diver on the outside of HMAS Swan, on a day of good visibility.

Inside the ship the light filters through the doors, but you need a torch to add colour and detail back to the corals growing on the walls. As you float through the corridors, you will recognise familar structures - kitchen benches, toilets, chairs and tables. In the bridge you can sit and look out into the blue waters beyond.

Schools of bullseyes often hang around the crows nest, and in the dark rooms of the interior. At the very bottom down near the propellor is sometimes a huge wobbegong. If you are very lucky, you might even see a grey nurse shark.

> Image: [4d96036e5b8b5e310940ea9c72287b79.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/4d96036e5b8b5e310940ea9c72287b79.jpeg) - Image: [089f07e22308ecfc19a03d4114b45053.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/089f07e22308ecfc19a03d4114b45053.jpeg) - Schools of bullseye around the crowsnest (left), a diver sitting in the bridge (right).

The crows nest is only about 7m underwater, and the bridge can be explored by Open Water Divers. If you are not yet qualified to 30m, some shops even offer the Advanced Open Water course on this wreck, which is a great deal.

You need a permit to access the wreck yourself, but it is easiest to do through a dive charter, or by joining a club like the [UWA Underwater Club](https://linktr.ee/uwauc).

> Image: [bb0f3739fb252cd306e546611c327e40.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/bb0f3739fb252cd306e546611c327e40.jpeg) - Image: [e8921edfb97713115d48752c026e3b5c-1.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2023/01/e8921edfb97713115d48752c026e3b5c-1.jpeg) - The toilets inside the ship (left), diver doing a safety stop on top of the crows nest at around 5m (right).

# Shipwrecks of the Swan River

Source: https://writing.padmorrison.com/swan-river-wrecks/
Published: 2022-12-29T01:00:50.000Z
Updated: 2022-12-29T08:40:06.000Z

The Swan River is full of shipwrecks, at last count more than 30. They are a favourite when the weather turns bad for ocean shore diving. This week I've chosen the Mayfield and City of Perth wrecks to snorkel, and Blackwall Reach to dive.

## The Mayfield and City of Perth Shipwrecks

*Rocky Bay, Fremantle. Depth 1-4m. Snorkel or Dive.
Slack tide (when currents are lowest).*

Rocky Bay is a spectacular example of a maritime landscape. The limestone cliffs were host to some of Fremantle's early industries, [many remnants of which still survive](https://museum.wa.gov.au/maritime-archaeology-db/sites/default/files/no._032_rocky_bay_fremantle_0.pdf?ref=writing.padmorrison.com).

> Image: [IMG_3556.JPG](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/IMG_3556.JPG) - Rocky Bay, looking over the old Burford's Soap Factory. The shipwrecks are beneath the cliffs.

The Mayfield and City of Perth shipwrecks lay next to each other, below the cliffs of Rocky Bay. Mayfield was a barge used to carry armour plating over to Rottnest Island for the guns at Oliver Hill, part of Perth's WWII defences. It wrecked in 1945. City of Perth was a paddle steamer, carrying cargo on the river itself. It is older than Mayfield, wrecked in 1890.

> Image: [IMG_3552-3.JPG](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/IMG_3552-3.JPG) - City of Perth and Mayfield shipwrecks from above. City of Perth is closest to the shore, with the Mayfield just visible further out.

On a clear day, the Mayfield can be seen from the surface. It can be snorkelled or dived from the shore, but is best done by boat. If you swim along the cliffs you will find the City of Perth wreck in just a metre of water. [Mayfield is just 15m out from there, in 4m of water.](https://goo.gl/maps/Ao4Js4PybV1B8W6w8?ref=writing.padmorrison.com)

> Image: [MayfieldWreck3D-20210108-pmSonyUW-0417.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/MayfieldWreck3D-20210108-pmSonyUW-0417.jpg) - Image: [MayfieldWreck3D-20210108-pmSonyUW-1130.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/MayfieldWreck3D-20210108-pmSonyUW-1130.jpg) - Divers on the Mayfield wreck.

When diving the Mayfield site [look for the two engines on either end](https://skfb.ly/6XZs9?ref=writing.padmorrison.com), the stern one for propulsion, and the bow one for a jib crane.

> Image: [Screenshot-2022-12-28-at-5.50.48-pm-1.png](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/Screenshot-2022-12-28-at-5.50.48-pm-1.png) - [3D Model of Mayfield](https://skfb.ly/6XZs9?ref=writing.padmorrison.com), December 2021.

## Blackwall Reach

*Bicton. Depth 5-18m. Dive.
All weather.*

Blackwall Reach is best known for the Blackwall Reach Barge. Like many wrecks in the river, it was sunk on purpose - abandoned by the The Goldfields Metal Mining Company in 1967. As always, [Dave Jackson has great advice on finding this wreck](https://www.divingwawrecks.com/blackwallreach?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) (under mooring 716), and the others nearby.

> Image: [c856e8391779b22ad195326aacd05ded.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/c856e8391779b22ad195326aacd05ded.jpeg) - Diving Blackwall Reach after heavy rains (July 2021). This is the in the middle of the day.

I enjoy diving the shipwrecks at Blackwall Reach, but there are some interesting sites closer into the cliffs. If you follow the 8m depth contour along to where people cliff-jump, you will find all sorts of dumped items - including cars.

Conditions can vary substanitally throughout the year. Sometimes, after heavy winter rains, the tannins washed downstream stain the water a very dark brown. This murky freshwater runs over the saltwater beneath, forming a cap that blocks out all the light. The water can be quite clear below this halocline (transition from fresh to salt), but you will need a torch.

> Image: [46a07a1c7ae5d2f766dae04549340882.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/46a07a1c7ae5d2f766dae04549340882.jpeg) - Image: [fded7c48b031e24afb6f285c7749da36-1.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/fded7c48b031e24afb6f285c7749da36-1.jpeg) - One of the Blackwall Reach cars

This stretch of the river is known for being the deepest - with a channel going past 18m. The very deepest point is just upstream, at 22m metres - the site of the Mosman Bay Barge. This is clearly where people go to get rid of trash... or things they want to hide. It is no longer possible to dump cars here, after a barrier was installed, so this is a record of insurance-frauds-past.

> Image: [a9af61a44670909efebd846b8bd5e631.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/a9af61a44670909efebd846b8bd5e631.jpeg) - Image: [c80e8cd72e4cafcbced43feef322ae72.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/c80e8cd72e4cafcbced43feef322ae72.jpeg) - Various dumped items at Blackwall Reach.

# Wrecks you haven't dived yet

Source: https://writing.padmorrison.com/wrecks-you-havent-dived-yet/
Published: 2022-12-22T01:00:40.000Z
Updated: 2022-12-29T08:42:38.000Z

This week we take a deeper dive into shipwrecks - and avoid the crowds at [Omeo and North Mole](https://writing.padmorrison.com/intro-to-shipwrecks/). Vergulde Draeck (also known as the Gilt Dragon shipwreck) is hundreds of years old, but rarely dived due to its remoteness. The Camilla shipwreck is a little bit closer to home, but isn't yet a popular dive site.

## Vergulde Draeck (Gilt Dragon)

*Ledge Point, the WA Midwest. Depth 8m. Dive or snorkel.
Very low swell (less than a metre). Easterly wind.*

Vergulde Draeck wrecked on a reef in 1656. It was sailing from the Netherlands to Batavia (now Jakarta), following the Brouwer Route along the 'Roaring Forties' - strong westerley winds just below 40°S latitude. Ships were meant to follow these winds across the Indian Ocean from the Cape of Good Hope in South Africa, before turning north towards Indonesia. Sometimes, they went a little bit too far, and hit Australia.

When the ship sunk, most on board drowned. Some, however, made it to shore, and a few even sailed up to Batavia (Jakarta) for help. Rescuers returned to search for the remaining survivors, but they were never found. Their fate remains a mystery.

> Image: [FD309403-8D70-4F34-906C-6C7F6F3A4E78.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/FD309403-8D70-4F34-906C-6C7F6F3A4E78.jpeg) - Cannon of the Vergulde Draeck.

Vergulde Draeck is one of four known VOC ships wrecked on the Western Australian coast. The VOC, or Dutch East India Company, was arguably the first multinational corporation. It built enormous wealth and military power, mainly by trading spices from the islands of Indonesia.

The wreck site itself is shallow and exposed to the harsh Indian Ocean swells, but on a calm day it makes for a beautiful dive. Cannons on the reef are the most distinct features. One of them is cemented upright at the entrance to a limestone cave system, which contains smaller debris from the wreckage.

> Image: [08D1CDDC-1C6C-4D27-8A7E-06301246AAA4.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/08D1CDDC-1C6C-4D27-8A7E-06301246AAA4.jpeg) - Image: [F34BBA1F-E42D-487A-93F6-69235A4446BB.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/F34BBA1F-E42D-487A-93F6-69235A4446BB.jpeg) - Gilt Dragon caves. In the entrance to one of the caves is an upright cannon (right).

### Protecting the wreck

Artefacts are still visible on the wreck site today, and are [still turning up on the nearby coastline](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2022-11-15/gilt-dragon-shipwreck-mystery-develops-with-bolo-balls-found/101598608?utm_campaign=abc_news_web&utm_content=link&utm_medium=content_shared&utm_source=abc_news_web). It is important to leave these artefacts alone and in-place, because they are valuable clues from the past that are easily destroyed if removed.

When the wreck was found in 1963, it was quickly looted by treasure hunters. In response, historic shipwrecks were legally protected by the WA Government, and they became property of the public, managed by the Museum.

In 1972, archaeologists from the newly-formed WA Museum Department of Maritime Archaeology excavated the wreck, in the first major underwater archaeological excavation in Australia. Take a visit to the WA Shipwrecks Museum in Fremantle, to see what can be learned from the shipwreck artefacts, about the people on board and the emerging global economy.

> Image: [8E6EF390-CB72-4BD9-8D32-C149B64B1246.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/8E6EF390-CB72-4BD9-8D32-C149B64B1246.jpeg) - Image: [9FC3A2F4-5BD2-48F1-B57E-F56636A52675.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/9FC3A2F4-5BD2-48F1-B57E-F56636A52675.jpeg) - Artefacts from the Vergulde Draeck wreck are still visible on site. Notice the rim of a beardman jug (right).

### Logistics for accessing the wreck site

The site can be accessed by boat from Two Rocks marina, or even by launching a tinny from Ledge Point. The museum database has GPS coordinates. It's a couple of hours north of Perth, but a manageable day trip.

Wait for exceptionally low swell. Waves break dangerously onto the reef, and will easily wreck your boat. Don't make the same mistake they did!

> Image: [faviconV2](https://www.museum.wa.gov.au/maritime-archaeology-db/wrecks/vergulde-draak-draeck-gilt-dragon?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) - Image: [wrecks_map-mid-west.png](https://www.museum.wa.gov.au/maritime-archaeology-db/wrecks/vergulde-draak-draeck-gilt-dragon?ref=writing.padmorrison.com)

> Image: [6BC2173C-8D81-4DBF-8677-E4929ADA4F70.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/6BC2173C-8D81-4DBF-8677-E4929ADA4F70.jpeg) - Shallow reef of the Vergulde Draeck (Gilt Dragon) wreck site. Shore in the background.

## Camilla

*Kwinana, near Alcoa Jetty. Depth 2-3 m. Snorkel or Dive.
Easterly wind.*

Camilla was a 2-masted brigantine, built in 1834 Scotland. It was used in Fremantle Harbour as a lighter, which is a type of vessel that carries cargo or passengers to moored ships. It was abandoned and sunk in 1903.

The wreck site is just 80m off Challenger Beach in Kwinana, and can be dived and snorkelled in most conditions. Dave Jackson has [great directions on his site](https://www.divingwawrecks.com/camilla?ref=writing.padmorrison.com), but in short I normally just [check for the wreck with Google Maps](https://goo.gl/maps/374sJbYCK5zXnBGBA?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) on my phone and line it up with the shore. It's approximately ~25m long, and laying east-west. If you have a drone, you can double check its location and get some nice shots while you're at it.

> Image: [6145E416-7FD0-49D2-A3CF-9342F4C48BF2.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/6145E416-7FD0-49D2-A3CF-9342F4C48BF2.jpeg) - Image: [A4535DF9-223B-49A8-95F7-1DCB837C3DF1.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/A4535DF9-223B-49A8-95F7-1DCB837C3DF1.jpeg) - Structure of Camilla. Some of the wreck timbers are exposed.

### Identifying shipwrecks

Now I want you to forgive me for confidently calling this wreck Camilla, because [the most recent report](https://museum.wa.gov.au/maritime-archaeology-db/maritime-reports/alcoa-jetty-wreck-survey-report-2005?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) concluded it was probably, *but not definitely,* Camilla (a.k.a Balls Lighter). They chose to call it "the Alcoa Jetty Wreck".

Maritime archaeologists inspect shipwrecks and try to match physical features with the historical record, using documents like insurance registers. They usually reason with things like hull construction materials, length, tonnage, and structural features that may indicate its intended use.

[Last year I captured a 3D model of t](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/camilla-1903-shipwreck-d045e8d73b67423d887383143cbf6e19?ref=writing.padmorrison.com)he site by combining hundreds of images, in a process called [photogrammetry](https://padmorrison.com/photogrammetry?ref=writing.padmorrison.com). This is now standard in maritime archaeology, because it gives researchers a detailed site plan to inspect for clues.

> Image: [camilla2.png](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/camilla2.png) - [3D model of the Camilla wreck](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/camilla-1903-shipwreck-d045e8d73b67423d887383143cbf6e19?ref=writing.padmorrison.com), created using 1136 imagers taken on the 13th of August 2021.

In the site plan you can see the exposed timbers and iron knees. The iron knees reinforced the timber hull of the vessel, and would have supported a deck. There are 26 knees in total, which means the vessel was probably surveyed to more than 200 tonnes. It also shows the approximate dimensions of the vessel, which almost, but doesn't exactly, match Camilla (which was 85.9 x 23.6 x 14.5 ft). When you dive, look out for the copper fastenings, which are further clues to its construction.

> Image: [904A4505-E4B1-485D-ADB8-774A6CD99970.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/904A4505-E4B1-485D-ADB8-774A6CD99970.jpeg) - Unidentified structural material on the Camilla wreck site.

### Industrial histories and futures

This sturdy working vessel speaks to the industrial history of Fremantle Port. The wreck now lays in the shadow of the Alcoa Kwinana Alumina Refinery, a huge facility that processes bauxite ore into alumina, which is then shipped elsewhere to manufacture aluminium.

As industries change (and sea levels rise) - I can't help wondering what this landscape will look like 100 or 200 years into the future.

> Image: [7E2D8ED4-9720-45CA-A6E6-5F6356EB85D2.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/7E2D8ED4-9720-45CA-A6E6-5F6356EB85D2.jpeg) - The Alcoa Kwinana Alumina Refinery, with the Camilla shipwreck in the foreground.

# Creatures

Source: https://writing.padmorrison.com/creatures/
Published: 2022-12-14T23:00:28.000Z
Updated: 2022-12-29T08:42:13.000Z

Sea creatures are everywhere, at every spot and in all conditions. If you're starting out, Mettam's Pool is a great snorkel, and Ammo Jetty is where you can find the better-camouflaged creatures such as octopuses and sea horses.

If you need more adrenaline, try last week's theme: **[Beasts](/beasts/)**.

## Mettams Pool

*Marmion Marine Park. Depth 0-5m. Snorkel.
Low swell (less than a metre). Easterly or northerly wind.*

Mettams Pool is one of Perth's favourite snorkelling spots. It's a small sheltered lagoon [just north of Trigg Beach](https://goo.gl/maps/E1qk2UZe4VedHahy7?ref=writing.padmorrison.com), in Marmion Marine Park. When the swell is low, and there is a nice easterly wind, you can expect a very pleasant and easy snorkel.

The sea life here is a great introduction to the usual characters of Perth waters. You can see most of them in the protection of the shallow lagoon, inside the reef in 0-2m of water. Keep a particular eye out for redlip morwong, buffalo bream, and the shellfish (including abalone).

> Image: [EE07714F-4BDD-4492-AC35-79BDAA7D89A7.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/EE07714F-4BDD-4492-AC35-79BDAA7D89A7.jpeg) - Swimming over the reef at Mettams Pool.

Before you visit, I recommend picking up a copy of *Common Marine life of Rottnest Island* by Britt Anderson, sold as a small 'bush book' in various outdoor shops and bookstores. This is focused on Rottnest, but covers much of what you will see along the coast, including at Mettams. Once you start to recognise different species, the ocean becomes a much more familar place.

A step up from there is *Wonders of Western Waters: The Marine Life of South-Western Australia*** **by Sue Morrison and Ann Storrie, which appears to now be out of print, and *The Perth Coast Fish Book* by Glen Whisson and Alexandra Hoschke*. *Plant-wise, *Marine Plants of the Perth Region* by John Huisman, Julia Phillips and Cheryl Parker is another 'bush book' that will have you telling your kelp from your sargassum in no time. For a comprehensive look into plants, *Marine Plants of Australia* by John Huisman is the bible - and has one of my all-time favourite book covers.

> Image: [452973FD-B7D0-454D-89C6-C9E0DC7ED2B9.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/452973FD-B7D0-454D-89C6-C9E0DC7ED2B9.jpeg) - Image: [BD77FA46-267E-4168-8538-1522B0867C2F.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/BD77FA46-267E-4168-8538-1522B0867C2F.jpeg) - Stingray (left), Redlip Morwong (right)

The main danger at this site can be the swell/surge over the reef - if the weather is bad, give this site a miss. On the outside of the reef it is a bit deeper, getting down to 3-4m. This is a great spot to practice duck diving, especially through some of the accessible swim-throughs. On your way out, there are changing and shower facilities just up the stairs.

> Image: [64DF03FF-02D5-4BA2-BA97-5C3CEAEB3E95.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/64DF03FF-02D5-4BA2-BA97-5C3CEAEB3E95.jpeg) - The deeper water on the outside of Mettams Pool.

## Ammo Jetty

*Woodman Point. Depth 0-9m. Dive or snorkel.
Low swell (less than a metre). Easterly or northerly wind.*

Ammo Jetty is a popular dive spot in Coogee, south of the [Coogee Maritime Trail](https://writing.padmorrison.com/intro-to-shipwrecks/). With easy beach access, plenty of parking, and lots of sea life - you will normally run into many other divers down there.

### Life on the pylons

The structure of the jetty pylons provides a habitat for marine creatures you will struggle to find elsewhere. Swim slow and look close, because many of them are very tiny. One of the hardest to see is the seahorse, but the trick is to look for the shape of their tail wrapped around an anchor point.

> Image: [49FA3449-8959-4B58-B7B0-28A739826456.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/49FA3449-8959-4B58-B7B0-28A739826456.jpeg) - Diver at Ammo Jetty looking a seahorse.

> Image: [DF97117D-A749-4674-9204-FF6440CF7341.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/DF97117D-A749-4674-9204-FF6440CF7341.jpeg) - Image: [B0786247-118B-4F8E-9BAA-218A5F708DA5.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/B0786247-118B-4F8E-9BAA-218A5F708DA5.jpeg) - Telesto coral on the pylons, with the bright white polyps on full display (left), and a seahorse camoflauged on another pylon (right)

### Octopus

The biggest draw of this site for many divers is the octopus. They makes dens in nooks and crannies, but often venture out for food or curiosity. You can spot them first by the pile of recently-eaten shell around the den entrance.

Most them are octopus djinda, meaning 'star' in Noongar. This [newly-named species](https://www.smh.com.au/environment/sustainability/a-star-is-spawned-wa-s-common-octopus-gets-new-celestial-noongar-name-20211220-p59j2e.html?ref=writing.padmorrison.com)was recently recognised as distinct from the gloomy octopus of the east coast.

> Image: [5C8156C1-37B3-471F-89CE-86B39179AEA3.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/5C8156C1-37B3-471F-89CE-86B39179AEA3.jpeg) - Octopus djinda, partly dropping its camoflage to inspect my camera lens.

You can interact with these incredibly curious creatures by gently offering your hand. If you are lucky, they will reach out with their tentacles and give you a little handshake - it's a very weird feeling. Be warned though - they will steal your camera if given a chance - and they are much stronger than they look!

> Image: [04863827-0A4D-4395-8F13-0CA71515A605.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/04863827-0A4D-4395-8F13-0CA71515A605.jpeg) - Image: [81AD93E4-9D11-46A2-BCE6-5EBCFE63B558.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/81AD93E4-9D11-46A2-BCE6-5EBCFE63B558.jpeg) - Octopus are curious creatures, and they will wrestle your camera from you if given a chance.

### Your first night-dive?

Jetty dives are a great way to start night diving. They are easy to navigate, and have plenty of creatures that are much more active at night. I've seen many octopus, squid, and even baby Port Jackson sharks at Ammo after dark. After diving the site during the day, you should head along with a dive club one evening after work.

> Image: [A4F2B066-D246-493E-87A6-A44A395DB856.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/A4F2B066-D246-493E-87A6-A44A395DB856.jpeg) - Ammo Jetty at sunset - fishing on top, diving below.

# Beasts

Source: https://writing.padmorrison.com/beasts/
Published: 2022-12-07T23:00:05.000Z
Updated: 2022-12-29T08:42:16.000Z

The ocean is full of wonderful animals - some of which are bigger than divers. This week we feature two adventures with slightly different fear factors: diving Shark Cave off Rottnest Island, and snorkelling with the sea lions off Carnac Island's eastern beach.

---

## Shark Cave, Rottnest

*Rottnest. Depth 25m. Advanced Dive.
Boat dive in low swell (less than a metre).*

Grey nurse sharks are gentle giants, growing to over 3m long. They can be found all along the coast - usually near rocky caves. Shark Cave is one of the most reliable spots for them near Perth. If you are lucky, you may see several sharks at once.

Dive charters visit when the weather is good - it is located off the west end of Rottnest Island and is very exposed, so you need low swells to dive it safely. The main entrance is through a small hole in the reef at about 15m depth. Descend through it carefully, and look around for sharks. The bottom of the cave is at 25m depth, so this is your excuse to get your Advanced Open Water Diver certification.

> Image: [0E8633E1-6E66-4540-BEF7-170B532B246A.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/0E8633E1-6E66-4540-BEF7-170B532B246A.jpeg) - Image: [F70189DB-302A-4516-9261-61E1C6366EA3.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/F70189DB-302A-4516-9261-61E1C6366EA3.jpeg) - The entrance to Shark Cave, which opens into a huge underwater cathedral

The silhouette of a grey nurse is instantly recognisable, with a long heterocercal tail. As they come closer, their teeth look fearsome - but they are for eating little fish, not humans. They can bite - but just don't annoy them, and you will be fine.

They swim slowly, until they decide not to. Most of the time they float in place, using their enormous oily liver to control their buoyancy. However, with one flick of the tail they can be gone. My biggest fear is getting whacked by one accidentally, and having to explain that I was slapped by a shark. Note they are quite skittish - so you should get there early, and make sure to keep your distance so they stick around for the divers after you.

> Image: [9283F225-9D15-404C-8AC5-49E1BA46702F.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/9283F225-9D15-404C-8AC5-49E1BA46702F.jpeg) - The distinctive tail of a Grey nurse shark.

### Photographing the sharks

Having extra light is important for photographing the sharks properly. My first Instagram shots were lit with a small 1,200 lumen hand torch, which was enough to fill in the detail of the sharks. Later I returned with two 4,000-lumen lights, and that made a huge difference - you can clearly see the spots that identify each individual.

If you are patient and gentle, the sharks will cruise right by you. Try and position yourself so they swim over the top of you to get a shot of their iconic teeth. A little bit of light goes a long way here.

> Image: [7D3109C3-3E67-4A90-97CF-B2EC25194760.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/7D3109C3-3E67-4A90-97CF-B2EC25194760.jpeg) - Image: [82FF0B0D-AB92-4F95-B4C4-93662C2BCDC7.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/82FF0B0D-AB92-4F95-B4C4-93662C2BCDC7.jpeg) - Grey nurse sharks lit by two 4,000-lumen video lights [(D40's from Nik Johnson's Dive Lantern - strong recommend)](https://perthscuba.com/products/v40-video-light-4200lm)

Make sure to get a few in natural light as well, with the sharks silhouetted against the opening of the cave. Have your dive buddy turn their torch on, which will make them stand out in photographs, and give a sense of scale to the enormous space.

---

## Sea Lions of Carnac Island

*Carnac Island. Depth 3m. Snorkel.
Boat access, well protected from westerly winds.*

The eastern beach of Carnac Island is a great place to swim with Australian sea lions. They are playful, and will often approach you in the water. There is nothing quite like trying to keep up as they race around you, splashing and leaping.

> Image: [B5E33EE6-3A11-416D-BEC3-3D8DB21D5FA8.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/B5E33EE6-3A11-416D-BEC3-3D8DB21D5FA8.jpeg) - Image: [91FF465A-8554-430B-968C-11DBAE990641.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/91FF465A-8554-430B-968C-11DBAE990641.jpeg) - Sea lions are curious creatures - let them approach you, but don't get in their personal space!

This is an easy snorkel in just a few metres of water, but you need a boat to access the island. [There are beaches on the eastern and western sides](https://goo.gl/maps/qaTzYAeYTd8XdfxX6), but I've only snorkelled on the east. You can anchor in the shallow bay just off the beach, and swim in from there. It's a much shorter trip than Rottnest, and offers comparable snorkelling.

> Image: [favicon.ico](https://exploreparks.dbca.wa.gov.au/park/carnac-island-nature-reserve?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) - Image: [canoeing-kayaking-icon.svg](https://exploreparks.dbca.wa.gov.au/park/carnac-island-nature-reserve?ref=writing.padmorrison.com)

On a safety note: respect their personal space. Do not get too close, and definitely do not approach them on land. If you get bitten, that will be very embarrassing.

> Image: [0969F4E0-5AE2-406C-80A7-21C3CD3BEA80.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/12/0969F4E0-5AE2-406C-80A7-21C3CD3BEA80.jpeg) - If you are lucky, the sea lions will come and play in pairs.

# Intro to Shipwrecks

Source: https://writing.padmorrison.com/intro-to-shipwrecks/
Published: 2022-11-30T23:00:40.000Z
Updated: 2022-12-29T08:42:44.000Z

Shipwrecks! We love them! Full of history and sea life. They are staples of diving and snorkelling in Perth - here are the two best to start with.

## Omeo Shipwreck and the Coogee Maritime Trail

*Coogee. Depth 2-7m. Snorkel or Dive.
Low swell (less than a metre). Easterly or northerly wind.*

The Coogee Maritime Trail is the best beginner snorkel spot in Perth. The trail is centred around the Omeo shipwreck, with artificial reef and sculptures to explore along the rock wall. There are stairs over the rock wall for easy access, near plenty of shade, grass and parking. You will see everyone from small children to experienced adult scuba divers on the wreck - it really is for everyone.

> Image: [de2eb3c6-a2b6-46fb-9bac-97d9e1fda030_rw_1920.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/11/de2eb3c6-a2b6-46fb-9bac-97d9e1fda030_rw_1920.jpg) - Image: [4c5ce321-95c4-456e-ba8a-53799f6766a6_rw_1920.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/11/4c5ce321-95c4-456e-ba8a-53799f6766a6_rw_1920.jpg) - The Omeo shipwreck in 2022.

### The Omeo Shipwreck

Omeo was used to transport cargo and passengers all over the world, under both sail and steam power. Notably, it helped build Australia's communications infrastructure: including the construction of the Bass Strait submarine cable (linking Tasmania to the mainland), and the Overland Telegraph line (linking Australia to Britain via Singapore). It also [famously destroyed the Swanspit Lighthouse in Port Phillip Bay](http://nla.gov.au/nla.news-article63185703?ref=writing.padmorrison.com).

> Image: [favicon.ico](https://museum.wa.gov.au/maritime-archaeology-db/wrecks/omeo?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) - Image: [wrecks_map-metro.png](https://museum.wa.gov.au/maritime-archaeology-db/wrecks/omeo?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) - More information on the Western Australian Museum's database

The ship was blown ashore during a storm in 1905, and has stayed stranded in the sand ever since. As the industrial landscape grew and changed around it, the wreck rusted and decayed. Now it rests just 20m from the rock wall, in a few metres of water - with only a bit of the stern sticking out above the waterline.

### Artificial reef, artefacts and sculptures

> Image: [3DCourse-20201129-pmDrone-0412-edited-2-1.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/11/3DCourse-20201129-pmDrone-0412-edited-2-1.jpg) - The Maritime Trail from above

The Maritime Trail follows the rock wall for about 180m, out from the shallows to about 7m depth. There are various different shapes of artifical reef (apollo, abitat, reef mats).  Look for the yellow signs along the wall, which tell you about how far apart everything is.

> Image: [apple-touch-icon.png](https://www.cockburn.wa.gov.au/Recreation-and-Attractions/Trails-Tracks-and-Lookouts/Coogee-Maritime-Trail?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) - Image: [GalleryHandler.ashx](https://www.cockburn.wa.gov.au/Recreation-and-Attractions/Trails-Tracks-and-Lookouts/Coogee-Maritime-Trail?ref=writing.padmorrison.com)

In the shallows, there are some artefacts with information plaques, including an anchor, a replica cannon, and a railway wheel from Elizabeth Quay. About half way out is a huge sea star sculpture with a swim-through. Towards the end are the spectacular 'Reef Pyramids', which are excellent for freediving practice.

> Image: [DSC00028-OmeoSnorkel-20220126-pmSonyUW-0028-edit.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/11/DSC00028-OmeoSnorkel-20220126-pmSonyUW-0028-edit.jpg) - Image: [DSC00023-OmeoSnorkel-20220126-pmSonyUW-0023-edit.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/11/DSC00023-OmeoSnorkel-20220126-pmSonyUW-0023-edit.jpg) - Anchor on display near the Omeo shipwreck, with an information plaque.

> Image: [DSC00084-OmeoSnorkel-20220126-pmSonyUW-0084-edit-5.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/11/DSC00084-OmeoSnorkel-20220126-pmSonyUW-0084-edit-5.jpeg) - Image: [f53383ec-e3b4-498e-9b84-5b3cb5aefd78_rw_1920-4.jpg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/11/f53383ec-e3b4-498e-9b84-5b3cb5aefd78_rw_1920-4.jpg) - Reef temple (left), abitat artifical reef (right)

If you're new to snorkelling, or just haven't been to Coogee yet - do it this weekend. It is best in low swell and with an offshore wind (light easterlies) - often this is in the morning before the sea breeze comes in. Keep an eye on local snorkelling and diving facebook groups - people will be posting when the conditions are good.

---

## North Mole Wreck

*Fremantle. Depth 10m. Dive or Freedive.
Low swell (less than a metre). Easterly or southerly wind.*

The North Mole wreck is another very accessible wreck site, if you are looking for something a bit deeper than Omeo. It makes an excellent quick morning dive, or an approachable first night-dive.

> Image: [IMG_1532-1.jpeg](https://writing.padmorrison.com/storage.ghost.io/c/ad/04/ad049ba7-9562-48cd-88a5-75b0ec92e783/content/images/2022/11/IMG_1532-1.jpeg) - The North Mole wreck on an average day.

The wreck is likely a Priestman Grab Dredge which sunk around 1928. These worked dredging channels in the Swan River and Fremantle Harbour at the beginning of the 20th Century, making it an important part of early Perth's transport infrastructure. During the construction of North Mole, the Museum moved it 200m north to its present position, so that it would remain accessible to divers. Most of the other wrecks in the area are now covered by reclaimed land. [Read more in Ian Warne's 2020 report to the Maritime Archaeological Association of Western Australia](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/56d7e469b6aa604e5de26ebf/t/5f3609fa697c8a3046289c8c/1597377087376/MAAWA+reports+2019-2020+volume+6.pdf?ref=writing.padmorrison.com).

> The North Mole wreck is visible from a drone.

The most risky bit of this site is the entry: you climb down rocks from [the fishing platform on the north side of the mole](https://goo.gl/maps/v53xJHkj9Tff1aX7A?ref=writing.padmorrison.com). That being said, it's relatively safe if you take it slow. Best to wait for swell at 1m or below, but the site is otherwise very well protected. In the water, there is a pole trail for divers, which will guide you 180m north to the wreck site. I strongly recommend [Dave Jackson's Diving Western Australia's Shipwrecks page on this](https://www.divingwawrecks.com/north-mole-barge?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) - which has a full guide including an excellent map.

> Image: [pfavico.ico](https://www.divingwawrecks.com/north-mole-barge?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) - Image: [1a3ee8_d5c9a07da4c44b27805889909f4e5227~mv2.jpg](https://www.divingwawrecks.com/north-mole-barge?ref=writing.padmorrison.com) - Dave Jackson's Diving Western Australia's Shipwrecks Page for North Mole

The site is also an excellent freedive. At 180m from shore and 10m depth, it is a similar trek to the reef temples at the end of the Coogee Maritime Trail – so if you can comfortably freedive around the bottom half of those, you can do North Mole without a tank. It certainly makes the climb down easier! The hardest bit is finding the wreck, but normally there are a few craypots on it, and you can swim towards the marker buoys.
